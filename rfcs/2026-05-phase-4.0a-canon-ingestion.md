## Context

The v2 runtime currently assumes **governance-first cognition**: every cycle through `GridOrchestrator.think()` invokes TruthEngine elemental gates and Woo's 0.97 compound judgment, then auto-writes to the MindGraph when truth passes.

That architecture presumes a dense substrate of canon against which to govern. The substrate does not yet exist.

A sparse graph cannot safely self-audit. Governance over an empty corpus is not governance — it is speculation wearing a robe.

This RFC seals the architectural pivot: **Phase 4.0a requires ingestion-first cognition.**

The first live cognition loop is not "What is drifting?" It is "Can the system correctly understand, classify, and place knowledge before trying to govern it?"

That is **epistemic structuring**. It is the real beginning of operational cognition.

## Purpose

Define the canonical first live cognition loop of MoStar Grid: a human-assisted ingestion pipeline that builds the canon substrate while training the system on interpretation, placement, and provenance — **without** granting any form of execution sovereignty.

## Scope

This RFC governs runtime behavior during Phase 4.0a only. It defines:

- The ingestion loop topology.
- The Decision Engine seam between TruthEngine and the graph.
- The human approval model and the five ingestion states.
- Provenance hierarchy and rollback semantics.
- Forbidden behaviors during 4.0a.
- Density promotion thresholds gating transition to Phase 4.0b.
- The Human Correction Law.

## Non-goals

This RFC explicitly does **not**:

- Define Phase 4.0b governance behavior beyond the promotion threshold.
- Define Phase 4.1+ workflow recommendation behavior.
- Modify TruthEngine threshold values for governance contexts.
- Authorize any autonomous execution path.
- Replace the prime law: **Woo interprets. TruthEngine governs. Grid executes.**

## Prime-law compliance check

- **Woo interprets** — Woo performs semantic and structural interpretation of incoming canon. Woo's role in 4.0a is amplified; its advisory output is the first signal the loop acts on.
- **TruthEngine governs** — TruthEngine's role is **softened but not removed**. In 4.0a it validates *consistency of interpretation against existing canon*, not *governance of execution*. See **TruthEngine Softened Role**.
- **Grid executes** — Grid's only execution in 4.0a is the act of writing an approved mutation to Neo4j after human seal. No interpretation, no governance.

Compliance: **PASS.**

## The Ingestion Loop

```
Human presents canon
  → Woo interprets structure + meaning
  → TruthEngine validates consistency (interpretation, not execution)
  → Decision Engine proposes ontology placement
  → Grid drafts graph mutations (proposed state)
  → Human reviews → approves | rejects | revises
  → On approve: Neo4j writes
  → Provenance stamped
  → Memory updated
```

No step in this loop bypasses the human gate.

## Ingestion States

Every ingestion candidate occupies exactly one of the following states. State transitions are append-only in the provenance ledger.

| State | Description | Next legal states |
|---|---|---|
| `proposed` | Woo has interpreted; TruthEngine has validated consistency; Decision Engine has drafted placement. Awaiting human review. | `approved`, `rejected`, `revised` |
| `approved` | Human has sealed the proposal as-is. Eligible for Neo4j write. | `committed` |
| `rejected` | Human has refused the proposal. No graph mutation occurs. Provenance retains the rejection for learning. | (terminal) |
| `revised` | Human has modified the proposal (placement, content, relationships, or interpretation). The revision becomes the new proposal. | `approved`, `rejected`, `revised` |
| `committed` | Mutation has been written to Neo4j. Provenance stamped. Memory updated. | (terminal) |

## Human Approval Model

- Every transition out of `proposed` requires explicit human action.
- Approval cannot be inferred from inaction, timeout, or absence of objection.
- Approval is recorded with the human's seal identifier and a timestamp.
- Revisions are first-class: a `revised` state is a new proposal, not an edit of the old. The provenance chain links revisions to their predecessors.
- The system **may** recommend approval based on TruthEngine + Decision Engine confidence, but recommendation is not authorization.

## Decision Engine Seam

A new component, **Decision Engine**, lives between TruthEngine and the graph. Its responsibilities:

- Receive Woo's interpretation and TruthEngine's consistency verdict.
- Propose ontology placement: which existing nodes the new canon should attach to, what relationship types should be created, what labels and properties should be set.
- Surface ambiguity explicitly. When multiple placements are plausible, present them ranked with reasoning.
- **Never** commit. Decision Engine produces only proposals.

The Decision Engine is the first component in the Grid architecture whose sole output is a structured suggestion to a human. Its quality is measured by human approval rates over time.

## TruthEngine Softened Role

During Phase 4.0a, TruthEngine operates in **consistency-validation mode**, not governance mode.

- The 0.97 compound Woo gate is **bypassed for ingestion**. Woo's interpretation is forwarded to TruthEngine for consistency scoring, not authorization.
- Elemental scores (Ikang 🜂, Mmọng 🜄, Afim 🜁, Isong 🜃) are computed and surfaced to the human, but **failure of any threshold does not block ingestion**. It annotates the proposal as low-confidence and elevates the human review priority.
- The full 0.97 governance gate is reserved for Phase 4.0b and beyond.
- TruthEngine retains absolute authority over execution-class decisions outside the ingestion loop. The softening applies **only** to the ingestion path.

This is the canon: in 4.0a, TruthEngine informs the human; the human governs.

| Threshold | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningful relationships | ≥ 10,000 | Count of `()-[r]->()` where `r` is not auto-generated provenance |

| Contradiction corpus | Present and characterized | Defined set of known-canon contradictions and their resolutions |

## Mutation Constraints

- **Only the `committed` transition causes graph writes.** No other state writes to Neo4j.
- Each commit is **atomic** at the Cypher level. Partial writes are rejected; the entire mutation succeeds or rolls back.
- Schema mutations (constraints, indexes, label additions) require their own RFC and are **forbidden** through the ingestion loop. See **Forbidden Behaviors**.
- Bulk ingestion is permitted but each item in the batch occupies its own ingestion-state record.

## Rollback Behavior

- **`proposed` → `rejected`** — no graph state change. Provenance retains the rejection record for learning. Cost: zero.
- **`proposed` → `revised`** — original proposal is sealed as superseded in provenance. Revision becomes the new proposal.
- **`committed` → undo** — requires a new ingestion cycle in the **reverse direction**. The original commit is not deleted; a counter-commit is sealed against it. The graph retains the full history. The provenance chain links the original and the counter-commit.
- No silent rollback. Every reversal is human-sealed and provenance-tracked.

## Provenance Hierarchy

Provenance records form a directed acyclic graph keyed on `cycle_id`. Hierarchy:

1. **Cycle** — one Talk→Decide→Commit attempt. Always has a `proposed` state record.
2. **Revision chain** — linked sequence of proposals where each `revised` state points to its predecessor.
3. **Approval seal** — human action that transitions `proposed | revised → approved`. Records sealer identity, timestamp, and any revision diff.
4. **Commit record** — Neo4j transaction identifier, written node and relationship IDs, and the elemental scores at time of commit.
5. **Counter-commit linkage** — if applicable, the cycle_id of the original commit being reversed.

All provenance is append-only. No edits. No deletes. New records may supersede old ones; old records remain readable.

## Runtime Boundaries

During Phase 4.0a:

- **Inbound:** the ingestion loop accepts canon presented through the human-gated API. No other write path to MindGraph is authorized.
- **Outbound:** the runtime may return interpretations, proposals, density telemetry, and provenance reads. It may **not** initiate external actions.
- **Sidechannels:** logging, metrics, and degraded-mode signals are permitted and expected.
- **Idempotency:** identical proposals submitted twice produce two `proposed` records with distinct cycle_ids. The human decides whether the second is a duplicate.

## Forbidden Behaviors During 4.0a

The following are **absolutely forbidden** during Phase 4.0a runtime. Any code path that enables them is a violation of canon and must be removed:

1. **Autonomous graph mutation** — no write to Neo4j without a human `approved` seal on the originating proposal.
2. **Schema mutation** — no `CREATE CONSTRAINT`, `CREATE INDEX`, label additions, or relationship-type introductions through the ingestion loop. Schema changes require their own RFC.
3. **Automatic ontology expansion** — Decision Engine may propose new node types or relationship types, but creation requires human approval and schema-RFC compliance.
4. **Self-generated provenance** — the system may not stamp moments, mark cycles approved, or write provenance entries that claim human action. Every provenance record marking human action must originate from a verified human seal.
5. **Self-modifying prompts** — DCX system prompts, Woo interpretation rules, and TruthEngine scoring heuristics may not be altered by the runtime. Changes require code commits and RFC review.
6. **Self-authorized execution** — no path from runtime cognition to action on external systems. Phase 4.0a is read-and-propose only. Execution sovereignty is granted by RFC, not by runtime confidence.

Violation of any forbidden behavior is a **fail-closed** event. The runtime must halt, log the violation, and require human re-authorization before resuming.

## Human Correction Law

> If human correction contradicts model interpretation, the correction becomes authoritative canon.

This is the **epistemic stability law** of MoStar Grid. It is not a configuration option. It is canon.

Operational implications:

- When a human revises Woo's interpretation, the revision is stored alongside the original interpretation. The revision is the canon. The original is the prior.
- TruthEngine's consistency model is updated against the human-corrected canon, not the model-original.
- Decision Engine's placement model is updated against human-approved placements, not its own original proposals.
- The provenance ledger preserves both the original model output and the human correction. The lineage is auditable forever.
- A pattern of corrections becomes training signal. The system learns from being wrong, but never overrides the correction.

This law makes long-term runtime safe. Without it, model drift accumulates silently. With it, every correction is a sealed teaching moment.

## Risks

1. **Human bottleneck** — every ingestion requires human approval. The loop is intentionally slow. Mitigation: batch review tooling, ranked proposals, and recommendation surfacing. Acceptable cost.
2. **Decision Engine quality** — early proposals will be weak. Mitigation: surface all reasoning, accept high rejection rates as training, do not optimize for approval rate prematurely.
3. **Provenance volume** — every state transition writes a record. The ledger will grow fast. Mitigation: provenance lives in its own subgraph with retention policy defined by a later RFC.
4. **Premature 4.0b promotion** — pressure to move faster than density warrants. Mitigation: thresholds are sealed in this RFC. Promotion requires a separate sealed RFC and a Flame Architect signature.
5. **Forbidden-behavior drift** — code changes over time may erode the fail-closed guarantees. Mitigation: tests in `tests/` must assert every forbidden behavior is blocked at the runtime boundary. CI must run these tests on every change.
